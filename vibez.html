01 · The idea

Campus feels smaller when everyone can find their people.

University life is full of conversations, interests, opportunities, and shared moments, but they are often scattered across group chats and platforms that were not designed around a campus.

I built Vibez as one student-centered space for campus life. Verified members can move between the wider feed, focused communities, private conversations, and casual games without losing the context of who the product is for.

02 · Product experience

One campus, with room for every side of student life.

Vibez combines broad campus discovery with smaller communities and mobile-first interactions, so students can choose how public, focused, or playful they want each visit to be.

03 · Building it

A social product with campus boundaries built into the system.

Vibez uses Vite and TypeScript for a responsive web application, with Capacitor packaging the same product for iOS and Android. The interface adapts from a desktop sidebar to a five-tab mobile navigation designed around everyday use.

Supabase supports verified email authentication, campus-scoped Postgres data, private messaging, realtime activity, storage, and Edge Functions. Server-side authorization keeps communities separated while moderation, blocking, reporting, and account controls support safer participation.

04 · What I learned

Community products need both freedom and clear boundaries.

Designing Vibez meant balancing open expression with campus identity, privacy, and safety. Anonymous participation can make students more comfortable speaking, but the product still needs strong reporting, blocking, moderation, and access rules underneath it.

The project also pushed me to connect many interaction models—feeds, polls, photos, messaging, community membership, study tools, multiplayer games, notifications, and marketplace features—without making the experience feel like a collection of unrelated apps.
