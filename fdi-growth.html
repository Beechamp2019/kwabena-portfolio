Foreign Direct Investment and Economic Growth in Sub-Saharan Africa

48 economies · 2000–2024

Research question

Is greater foreign direct investment associated with faster GDP per capita growth in Sub-Saharan African economies?

Project overview

Using World Bank World Development Indicators, I constructed a panel dataset covering 48 Sub-Saharan African economies from 2000 to 2024. I used Stata to examine the relationship between foreign direct investment and GDP per capita growth through OLS, multivariate regression, country fixed effects, clustered standard errors, and an outlier robustness test.

Conclusion

The analysis finds no robust evidence that higher FDI inflows were associated with faster GDP per capita growth across Sub-Saharan African economies from 2000 to 2024. The estimated relationship became negligible after accounting for economic controls and country-specific effects and remained insignificant after excluding extreme FDI observations. Domestic investment and trade openness showed stronger positive associations with growth in the later specifications.

Interpretation

Key Findings

Primary finding

The simple OLS model suggested a positive relationship between FDI and economic growth, but the estimate was not statistically significant. After introducing economic controls and country fixed effects, the estimated FDI relationship moved close to zero and remained statistically insignificant. The conclusion was unchanged after excluding extreme FDI observations.

Secondary finding

Domestic investment and trade openness showed stronger positive associations with growth in later specifications.
